Who are you?

The case

Atlas Packing House

Read it once, calmly (3 minutes). You will come back to it all along.

The business. Atlas Packing House washes, sorts and packs oranges for supermarkets and exporters. Day shift: 06:00 to 14:00, about 40 people, four packing lines. Every truck must leave by 14:00. Boxes are packed per client order (client, size, number of boxes).

The idea (nothing is built). A shared board at each packing line that tells the team which client order to pack next, which size, how many boxes, and that makes last-minute changes reach everyone. Not a screen design yet: only an idea.

The people.

  • Fatima, 45, line supervisor. Decides which order each line packs. Reads Arabic and French.
  • Rachid, 31, packer. Wet, gloved hands. Works 3 to 5 metres from where information could be shown. Reads French less easily; numbers are fine.
  • Samir, 38, forklift driver. Moves pallets to the trucks.
  • Lahcen, 50, quality controller. Rejects fruit that does not meet the standard.
  • Nora, 36, order planner, in the office. Takes client changes by phone and e-mail until 08:30.
  • Anti-persona: the owner's cousin, who wants charts and a dashboard. He never works in the hall.

The place and the limits. Cold, wet hall (about 12 °C). Noise around 85 dB (machines). Wet or gloved hands; hygiene rules (hairnets, no dirty hands on shared surfaces). Weak Wi-Fi near the washing area. Staff read Arabic and French, and some read little. Budget for the whole tool: 3,000 euros. It must be live in 2 months.

How it works today. Fatima writes the day plan on paper sheets taped to each line. Changes are shouted across the hall. Repacked boxes: about 5% of boxes. Late trucks: about 4 per month. Targets: repack at most 2%, late trucks at most 1 per month.
